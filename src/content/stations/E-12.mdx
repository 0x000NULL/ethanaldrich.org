## A degree taken while doing the job

B.S. Cybersecurity and Information Assurance at Western Governors University,
approved for graduation, expected December 2026. I took it while working as CTO
for two companies, which is the only reason it is worth writing about: the
coursework and the day job kept landing on the same problems from opposite
directions.

## Why competency-based mattered here

WGU does not award credit for seat time. Each course ends in a proctored
assessment or a project that gets defended against a rubric, and you move when
you can demonstrate the competency, not when the term ends.

For someone already doing the work that cuts both ways, honestly. Material I
used daily — networking, Windows and Linux administration, incident response —
I could move through quickly, because I could already demonstrate it. Material I
had *not* touched went slowly and there was nowhere to hide, because there is no
attendance mark to offset a failed assessment.

The parts that were genuinely new to me were the formal ones: writing a risk
assessment to a standard rather than to taste, and the governance and compliance
material that I had only met previously through the narrow lens of a PCI DSS
audit.

## Where the degree and the job met

- **Secure software.** I write and own the applications this business runs on, so
  the secure-development material was immediately testable against my own code
  rather than a toy example.
- **Digital forensics.** I had been doing endpoint investigations already, from
  acquisition through a root-cause write-up. The coursework gave me the order of
  operations and the chain-of-custody discipline I had been approximating.
- **Governance.** The weakest area coming in, and the one that most changed how I
  document decisions.

## Honest scope

The degree is bundled with several industry certifications, which is part of why
the Education line has the run of CompTIA stations it does. It is an
undergraduate degree earned largely remotely and asynchronously, not a research
programme, and I would not present it as one.

An M.S. on the same model is planned, which is the dashed branch off this
terminus. Planned means planned: not started, not enrolled.
