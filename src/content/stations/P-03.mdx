## Two boats, no second visit

Full network and AV integration on an **Ocean Alexander 32E** and a
**Princess Y72**: UniFi switching and Wi-Fi, VLAN segmentation, PoE, Control4
integration, and dual-Starlink failover. Including the fabricated mounts and the
on-site install.

A yacht is an unusually strict environment to build a network in. There is no
running a new cable next week, no spare rack space, and no popping back to swap a
switch. Everything has to be specified correctly the first time, fit in the space
that actually exists, and survive vibration, salt air and power that is not
always clean.

## The network

VLAN segmentation matters more here than in a typical install of this size,
because the traffic genuinely is different in kind:

- guest and owner devices
- AV control and media distribution
- vessel systems and cameras

Keeping those apart is straightforward Ethernet practice, and on a boat it is
also the difference between a guest's laptop being a guest's laptop and a guest's
laptop being on the same broadcast domain as vessel systems.

**Dual Starlink with failover** is the connectivity story. One uplink at sea is
not an uplink, it is a single point of failure that happens to work most of the
time.

PoE feeds the access points and cameras, which keeps the cable count down; on a
vessel, every cable you do not run is space and weight you do not spend.

## The fabrication

The mounts were fabricated rather than bought. Marine installs rarely offer a
flat surface in the place the hardware needs to be, and an off-the-shelf bracket
that almost fits becomes a rattle, then a crack, then a callout.

This is the part of the work that does not show up in a stack list: welding, CAD
and 3D printing are as load-bearing on this project as the switch configuration.
Both boats went into service on the first install.
