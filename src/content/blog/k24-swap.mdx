The Honda Beat is a mid-engine kei car: the engine sits behind the seats, ahead
of the rear axle, and the whole car is built around a 656cc three-cylinder making
63 horsepower. Putting a K24 in it is not a drop-in swap in any sense. It is a
fabrication project that happens to end with an engine in it.

## Why mid-engine changes the problem

In a front-engine car, an engine swap is mostly mounts, wiring and plumbing
within a bay that was designed to hold an engine of roughly that shape. Here the
bay was designed around an engine less than a third of the K24's displacement,
and everything structural in that area is load-bearing for a chassis that is
about half the weight of the target power.

So the work splits into three, and only one of them is about the engine:

1. **Structure** — making room without giving up chassis rigidity
2. **Drivetrain** — getting drive to the wheels through a gearbox never intended
   for this torque
3. **Everything downstream** — cooling, fuel, exhaust routing, brakes, and the
   fact that the weight distribution has changed substantially

## Where it stands

- Engine and transmission sourced: K24A with a 6-speed
- Rear subframe removed
- Custom engine mount design in progress

More than it looks like. Both are the same discipline: work out what the
constraint actually is before buying parts, and be honest about the failure mode
you are designing for. The difference is that a bad decision here is visible in
metal instead of a log file.
