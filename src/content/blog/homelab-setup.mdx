The homelab has been running since 2020, and its real job is not the services on
it. Almost everything I have put into production at work was broken here first,
on hardware I own, where the failure costs nothing but an evening. That framing
is worth stating up front, because it explains most of the choices below: this
cluster is optimised for *being rebuilt*, not for uptime.

## What the cluster looks like

Proxmox VE across multiple nodes, mixing full VMs and LXC containers depending on
what the workload actually needs, with Docker for the service tier, Nginx as
reverse proxy, and Tailscale for access.

## Storage

ZFS for redundancy, with local-lvm for container storage.

ZFS is the decision I would defend hardest. Snapshots make "try it and roll back"
a thirty-second operation, which is the entire point of a lab, and checksumming
means a failing disk announces itself rather than quietly corrupting things.

## Access: no open ports

Tailscale, so nothing has to be published to the open internet to be reachable.
This is the single highest-value decision in the whole build. A homelab with
forwarded ports is an internet-facing estate that nobody is paid to defend; a
homelab on a mesh VPN is just a private network that happens to be at my house.
